# MyGenie Coupon & Offers Playbook

### A Practical Guide for Restaurant Owners and Managers

*Version 1.0 | May 2026*

## 1. Introduction

Welcome to the MyGenie Coupon & Offers Playbook.

This guide explains the different kinds of coupons and offers you can run at your restaurant using MyGenie. Whether you manage a cozy cafe, a busy quick-service chain, a fine-dining establishment, or a cloud kitchen, this playbook will help you pick the right offers for your goals.

Each coupon type is explained in plain English with real-world examples, pricing in Indian Rupees, and practical tips. Think of this as your go-to reference whenever you're planning a new campaign.

---

## 2. Why Coupons Matter for Restaurants

Running the right coupon at the right time can transform your business:

- **Fill empty tables during slow hours.** A well-timed happy-hour offer can turn your quietest afternoon into a buzzing one.
- **Bring customers back.** A "Welcome Back" flat discount re-engages guests who haven't visited in weeks.
- **Increase bill size.** "Buy 2, get 1 free" encourages customers to add one more item to their order.
- **Move slow-selling items.** A targeted discount on a specific dish or category clears inventory and introduces customers to something new.
- **Reward your regulars.** Repeat-visit offers like "Every 5th coffee free" make loyal guests feel appreciated.
- **Launch new items with confidence.** An introductory discount on a new menu addition reduces the customer's risk of trying something unfamiliar.

The key is matching the right coupon type to your specific business goal. This playbook shows you how.

## 4. Coupon Types Explained

### 4.1 Flat Discount Coupon

**What it means:** A fixed Rupee amount is taken off the customer's total bill when conditions are met.

**How it works:**
- You set a coupon code (e.g., `FLAT100`).
- You set a minimum order value (e.g., ₹999).
- When a customer's order is ₹999 or above and they use the code, exactly ₹100 is deducted.

**Example Campaign:**

> **"Grand Opening Week"**
> Coupon: `WELCOME100`
> ₹100 off on all orders above ₹599.
> Valid for 7 days. One use per customer.

**Best for:** Dine-in restaurants, cloud kitchens, hotel room service.

**Good practices:**
- Always set a minimum order value. Without it, a customer could get ₹100 off a ₹150 order, which hurts your margins.
- Use round numbers (₹50, ₹100, ₹200) -- they're easier to remember.

**Risk if used badly:** If the minimum order is too low relative to the discount, you'll lose money on every redemption. A ₹100 discount on a ₹200 order is 50% off -- probably not what you intended.

---

### 4.2 Percentage Discount Coupon

**What it means:** A percentage of the total bill is deducted, but you set a maximum cap so your discount doesn't spiral out of control on large orders.

**How it works:**
- You set a coupon code (e.g., `SAVE10`).
- You set the percentage (e.g., 10%).
- You set a maximum discount cap (e.g., ₹150).
- On a ₹800 order, the discount is ₹80 (10% of ₹800). On a ₹2,000 order, the discount caps at ₹150.

**Example Campaign:**

> **"Weekday Lunch Special"**
> Coupon: `LUNCH10`
> 10% off on orders above ₹500. Maximum discount ₹150.
> Valid Mon-Fri, 12 PM to 3 PM. 3 uses per customer per month.

**Why the cap matters:** Without a maximum cap, a 10% coupon on a ₹10,000 corporate party bill becomes a ₹1,000 discount. The cap protects your revenue on large orders while still giving a meaningful discount on average-sized ones.

**Best for:** All restaurant types. Especially effective for repeat-use campaigns.

---

### 4.3 Item-Level Coupon

**What it means:** The discount applies only to a specific item on your menu, not the entire bill.

**How it works:**
- You choose which menu item the coupon applies to (e.g., Cappuccino).
- The discount can be a flat amount per item (₹50 off each Cappuccino) or a percentage (20% off each Cappuccino).
- Only the matching items in the order receive the discount. Everything else is full price.

**Example Campaigns:**

> **"Cappuccino Week"**
> 20% off on all Cappuccinos. No minimum order.

> **"Try Our New Burger"**
> ₹50 off the Smoked BBQ Burger. Valid for 2 weeks.

**Best for:** Cafes (pushing signature drinks), QSR (promoting new items), cloud kitchens (clearing slow movers).

**Why it's useful:**
- You can discount only items where you have healthy margins.
- It helps introduce customers to items they haven't tried.
- The rest of the bill is unaffected, so your overall revenue impact is predictable.

**Note:** This coupon type requires that your POS sends item details (which items are in the cart). If your POS doesn't send item data yet, ask the MyGenie team about setup.

---

### 4.4 Category-Level Coupon

**What it means:** The discount applies to an entire section of your menu, such as "Beverages," "Desserts," or "Starters."

**How it works:**
- You choose which menu category the coupon covers.
- Every item in that category gets the discount.
- The discount can be flat per item or a percentage.

**Example Campaigns:**

> **"Dessert Festival"**
> 15% off on all Desserts this weekend.

> **"Beverage Bonanza"**
> ₹30 off on every Beverage in the menu.

**Best for:** Cafes, bars, restaurants with a strong category identity.

**Why it's useful:**
- Promotes an entire section instead of one item.
- Great for themed promotions ("Soup Week," "Mocktail Month").
- Encourages customers to explore the category.

**Note:** Like item-level coupons, this requires POS item/category data.

---

### 4.5 Happy-Hour / Time-Window Coupon

**What it means:** Any of the coupon types above can be restricted to specific days and times. This turns a regular coupon into a happy-hour or time-limited offer.

**How it works:**
- You set which days the coupon is valid (e.g., Monday through Friday).
- You set the start time and end time (e.g., 4:00 PM to 7:00 PM).
- Outside those hours, the coupon simply doesn't work. No error, no confusion -- it just shows as "Available from 4 PM" to the cashier.
- The system uses your restaurant's local time (e.g., India Standard Time).

**Example Campaigns:**

> **"Happy Hour Beverages"**
> 20% off all Beverages, Monday to Friday, 4 PM to 7 PM.

> **"Late Night Pizza Deal"**
> Buy 1 Pizza, Get 1 Free. Friday and Saturday, 10 PM to 1 AM.

> **"Breakfast Rush Reward"**
> Every 3rd Coffee is free. Weekdays, 8 AM to 11 AM.

**Best for:** Cafes (afternoon slump), bars (classic happy hour), restaurants (filling slow meal periods), hotels (off-peak room service).

**Overnight windows are supported.** A "10 PM to 2 AM" window correctly spans midnight.

**What your cashier sees:** During off-hours, the coupon appears greyed out with a note like "Available from 4:00 PM." During valid hours, it works normally.

---

### 4.6 BOGO Coupon (Buy One, Get One)

**What it means:** When a customer buys one of a specific item, they get another one free (or at a discount).

**How it works:**
- You pick the item (e.g., Margherita Pizza).
- When the customer orders 2 Margheritas, the cheapest one is free.
- If they order 4, they get 2 free.
- You can limit how many times this repeats per order.

**Important:** The "free" item must already be in the customer's order. MyGenie does not add items to the cart automatically -- the customer or cashier must add both items before the discount applies.

**Example Campaigns:**

> **"BOGO Tuesdays"**
> Buy 1 Margherita Pizza, Get 1 Free. Every Tuesday.

> **"Coffee BOGO Mornings"**
> Buy 1 Americano, Get 1 Free. Weekdays 8 AM-11 AM.

**Benefit options:**
- **Free:** The second item is completely free.
- **Percentage off:** The second item is 50% off.
- **Flat amount off:** ₹50 off the second item.

**Best for:** Pizzerias, cafes, bakeries, dessert shops. Any restaurant where customers naturally order in quantities.

---

### 4.7 Buy-X-Get-Y Coupon

**What it means:** This is a more flexible version of BOGO. The "buy" item and the "get" item can be different.

**How it works:**
- You set the "buy" requirement: e.g., buy 2 Pizzas.
- You set the "get" reward: e.g., get 1 Garlic Bread free.
- The customer must have both the buy items and the get item in their order.
- The system checks quantities and applies the reward automatically.

**Example Campaigns:**

> **"Pizza Night Bundle"**
> Buy 2 Pizzas, Get 1 Garlic Bread free.

> **"Coffee & Cake"**
> Buy 3 Coffees, Get 1 Slice of Cake 50% off.

> **"Family Feast"**
> Buy 2 Biryanis, Get 1 Raita free.

**Important notes:**
- The get item must already be in the cart. The system does not add it automatically.
- If the "get" item costs more than expected, the discount still caps at the item's actual price (you'll never go negative).
- You can allow this to repeat: if someone orders 4 Pizzas, they get 2 Garlic Breads free (if `allow_repeat` is on).

**Best for:** Restaurants that want to cross-sell (e.g., main course + side dish), QSR meal combos, cafes (coffee + pastry pairing).

---

### 4.8 Every-Nth Item Coupon

**What it means:** For every N items of a kind that a customer orders, one is rewarded. Think of it as a quantity-based loyalty offer within a single order.

**How it works:**
- You set the number N (e.g., every 5th).
- You choose which items qualify (e.g., any Coffee).
- When a customer orders 5 Coffees, the cheapest one is free.
- If they order 10, two are free. The math is simply: `number of rewards = total qualifying items divided by N` (rounded down).

**How the counting works (by quantity, not order):**
- 4 Coffees: 0 rewards (not enough yet)
- 5 Coffees: 1 reward
- 9 Coffees: 1 reward (9 divided by 5 = 1, rounded down)
- 10 Coffees: 2 rewards
- 14 Coffees: 2 rewards
- 15 Coffees: 3 rewards

**The reward can be:**
- **Free:** The qualifying item is 100% off.
- **Percentage off:** e.g., every 3rd Dessert is 50% off.
- **Flat amount off:** e.g., every 4th Beverage is ₹50 off.

**Category-level is supported too:** "Every 5th Beverage free" counts all drinks together (3 Coffees + 2 Teas = 5 Beverages = 1 free).

**You can control:**
- **Maximum rewards per order:** e.g., "Every 5th Coffee free, max 2 free per order."
- **Repeat allowed or not:** If repeat is off, only one reward per order regardless of quantity.
- **Which unit gets the discount:** By default, the cheapest qualifying item. You can set it to the most expensive one instead.

**Example Campaigns:**

> **"Coffee Lover's Reward"**
> Every 5th Coffee is free. No limit per order.

> **"Dessert Happy Order"**
> Every 3rd Dessert is 50% off.

> **"Beverage Bulk Deal"**
> Every 4th Beverage is ₹50 off. Category: all Beverages.

**Best for:** Cafes (high-volume beverage orders), bakeries (dozen deals), corporate catering, QSR (large group orders).

## 7. How to Choose the Right Coupon

Ask yourself these five questions:

| Question | Recommended Coupon Type |
|---|---|
| **Do I want to increase total bill size?** | Flat discount with minimum order, Buy-X-Get-Y |
| **Do I want to move a specific item?** | Item-level discount, BOGO on that item |
| **Do I want more customers during slow hours?** | Happy-hour / time-window coupon |
| **Do I want to reward high-volume buyers?** | Every-Nth item |
| **Do I want a simple, universally appealing offer?** | Percentage discount with a cap |

**When in doubt, start with a percentage discount with a cap.** It's the most flexible and easiest to control.

## 8. Best Practices

1. **Always set a minimum order value.** Even ₹200 prevents abuse on tiny orders.

2. **Always set a maximum discount cap on percentage coupons.** A 20% coupon without a cap can lead to ₹1,000+ discounts on large party orders.

3. **Set expiry dates.** Open-ended coupons get forgotten or abused months later.

4. **Set per-customer limits.** "3 uses per customer" prevents one person from draining your promotion budget.

5. **Match the discount to your margins.** If an item has a 40% margin, a 20% discount is safe. A 50% discount means you're selling below cost.

6. **Use happy-hour coupons for slow periods.** Don't discount your peak hours -- those tables fill themselves.

7. **Use item/category coupons to push high-margin products.** Discount the items you earn most on, not your already-thin-margin staples.

8. **Keep offers simple.** If your cashier can't explain the offer in one sentence, simplify it.

9. **Decide upfront: can this coupon combine with loyalty points?** If not, configure it as non-stackable. If your best customers use loyalty, consider allowing it.

10. **Test one campaign before scaling.** Run a coupon for one week or at one location before rolling it out chain-wide. Measure the impact first.

11. **Track coupon performance.** MyGenie provides analytics showing how many times each coupon was used and how much discount was given. Review these numbers weekly.

## 9. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Unlimited percentage discounts without a cap.** A "20% off everything" coupon on a ₹8,000 corporate dinner is ₹1,600 off. Set a cap.

2. **Discounting your lowest-margin items.** If rice dishes already run at thin margins, discounting them further hurts. Discount beverages or desserts where margins are healthier.

3. **Confusing BOGO rules.** If your staff can't explain "Buy 2 Pizzas, Get 1 Garlic Bread Free" smoothly, the promotion will cause billing confusion. Train your team.

4. **Running too many coupons at once.** Three active coupons with overlapping conditions will confuse your cashier and customers. Run 1-2 focused campaigns at a time.

5. **Coupons that staff cannot explain.** If it takes three sentences to describe an offer, it's too complex. Simplify it.

6. **Not setting expiry dates.** A "summer special" that still works in December is a liability.

7. **Overlapping offers without testing.** If you have a "10% off everything" and a "BOGO Coffee" running simultaneously, make sure they don't stack in unexpected ways (MyGenie enforces one coupon per order, but customer expectations may differ).

8. **Not checking profitability.** A coupon that gets used 500 times at ₹100 off each is a ₹50,000 investment. Make sure the incremental revenue justifies it.

9. **Forgetting the get item.** For BOGO and Buy-X-Get-Y, the free or discounted item must be in the order. If a customer expects a free Garlic Bread but didn't add one to their order, the coupon won't apply. Train your staff to remind customers.

## 12. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Coupon code** | The text code a customer or cashier enters to apply a discount (e.g., `FLAT100`, `BOGO2026`). |
| **Minimum order value** | The smallest bill amount required for the coupon to work. |
| **Maximum discount cap** | The highest discount amount a percentage coupon can give, regardless of bill size. |
| **Usage limit** | Total number of times a coupon can be used across all customers. |
| **Per-customer limit** | Maximum times a single customer can use this coupon. |
| **Stackable with loyalty** | Whether this coupon can be used on the same order where loyalty points are also redeemed. |
| **Time window** | The days and hours during which a coupon is valid. |
| **BOGO** | "Buy One, Get One" -- a coupon where buying one item earns the same item free or discounted. |
| **Buy-X-Get-Y** | A coupon where buying X items of one kind earns Y items of another kind free or discounted. |
| **Every-Nth** | A coupon where every Nth item of a kind is free or discounted within a single order. |
| **Eligible item** | A menu item that qualifies for a coupon's discount. |
| **Category** | A menu section like "Beverages," "Desserts," "Starters." |
| **Channel** | Where the order is placed: POS (in-store), dine-in, takeaway, or delivery. |
